1.0.0

Latest 26 September 2026

The first public release, and the first on the Microsoft Store.

What iVault does

  • Video to a streaming link. Drop in a video file and iVault encodes it to HLS at 360p, 480p, 720p and 1080p with FFmpeg, uploads it, checks every file is reachable, and gives you one master.m3u8 link for any player or website.
  • Storage you already have. Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2 and MinIO, a local folder or NAS, Telegram, GitHub (served through jsDelivr), Google Drive, OneDrive and WebDAV. Use several and pick one per video; move a video from one to another later.
  • A whole season at once. Group episodes into batches, convert several videos in parallel, and pause and resume a job or a whole batch.
  • Subtitles and posters. Attach .srt or .vtt subtitles in any language and a picture of your own. Add them later to a video that is already published.
  • A catalog for your site. Organise episodes into series with English and Khmer titles, look up details on TMDB, translate to Khmer with Gemini using your own key, and export the catalog.
  • Telegram without extra setup. A Telegram channel works with just a bot token and a channel ID, and iVault plays those videos in its own preview.

Fixed

  • A failed Telegram upload now resumes with only the files that are left, instead of sending the whole video again.
  • Converting on Windows works: two Windows-only problems made FFmpeg stop with "Permission denied".

Good to know

  • On Windows, a pause takes effect after the current step (for example, once encoding finishes) rather than instantly.
  • Telegram accepts files up to 20 MB each, so iVault publishes one quality there instead of several.
  • Removing a video from iVault, or moving it to other storage, does not delete the files already uploaded.
  • A Google Drive connection needs renewing every 7 days while your Google OAuth app is in testing mode; OneDrive about every 90 days.

Earlier builds were internal previews and were not published.
