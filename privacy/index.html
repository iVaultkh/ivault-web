iVault Privacy policy

Your videos and keys never pass through us.

iVault is a desktop app with no account and no server of its own. This policy sets out what stays on your computer, what goes to the services you connect, and what reaches us: nothing.

  • Effective 26 September 2026
  • Version 1.0
  • iVault for Windows, macOS and Linux, including the Microsoft Store version
  • No account with us

    You never sign up, and we never learn who you are.

  • No telemetry

    No analytics, crash reports, ads or tracking code.

  • Keys in your keychain

    Every credential is kept by your operating system, never in plain files.

  • Never sold or shared

    We can't sell or share your data: we never receive it.

Who this covers

This policy covers the iVault desktop app, published by iVaultkh (ichamrong) under the MIT License, on every platform and in every store where it is offered. It also covers this website: static pages hosted on GitHub Pages, with no cookies, forms, analytics or third-party requests. Even its fonts are served from this site.

What we collect

Nothing. We do not run a service that receives data from the app. iVault contains no telemetry, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code, and it does not create a user account with us.

What stays on your computer

  • Your library and jobs: file paths, series and episode details, upload progress and the links iVault produced, in a local database in the app's data folder.
  • Encoded video: temporary HLS files while a job runs.
  • Credentials: storage access keys, bot tokens, OAuth refresh tokens and API keys, stored in your operating system's secure credential store (Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain or the Linux Secret Service).

Where your data goes

iVault only makes the network requests needed for features you turn on. Data travels from your computer straight to the service, under that service's privacy policy and an account that belongs to you.

Your content

You are responsible for the videos you upload and for having the right to store and share them. Links iVault creates are as public as the storage you point them at: a public bucket or public repository makes the video reachable by anyone with the link.

Your choices

  • Remove a service: delete the provider in iVault's Storage screen, and its credentials are deleted from your keychain with it.
  • Remove everything: uninstall the app and delete its data folder.
  • Requests to us: because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to export, correct or delete. For data held by a service you connected, use that service's own tools.

Security

Credentials are kept in the OS credential store and are removed from error messages and logs before they are shown. Connections to services use HTTPS where the service offers it.

Children

iVault is a tool for publishing video and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts handling data differently, this page is updated before that version ships, and the change is listed here.
